Simple Physical Meaning of the First-Principles Derivation of the Five Physical Domains (SOMT)

Throughout history, human philosophy and science have attempted to categorize the fundamental building blocks of reality---often looking at the universe through distinct domains such as space, motion, energy, fluids/fields, and solid matter. Traditional physics treats these domains as separate phenomena governed by different laws and forces.

The First-Principles Derivation of the Five Physical Domains bridges this gap entirely. It proves that these five domains are not independent physical substances, but rather successive organizational regimes of one single, unified substrate:

\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \text{Substrate (Ākāśa)} &\longrightarrow \text{Propagation (Vāyu Domain)}\\ &\longrightarrow \text{Transformation (Agni Domain)}\\ &\longrightarrow \text{Continuum (Jala Domain)}\\ &\longrightarrow \text{Stable Material Organization (Pṛthvī Domain)} \end{aligned} } \]

The Five Successive Transitions

Substrate to Propagation Transition (Ākāśa → Vāyu Domain):

Starting from the foundational master field equation (\(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sub}}\Psi=0\)), any local disturbance (\(\delta\Psi = \Psi - \Psi_0\)) creates an organized transmission of change. This linear wave evolution yields coherent propagating modes \(\Phi(x,\lambda)\) with a distinct phase velocity (\(v_{\mathrm{prop}} = \omega/\lvert k\rvert\)), giving birth to the Propagation Domain (movement and wave transmission).

Propagation to Transformation Transition (Vāyu Domain → Agni Domain):

As propagating disturbances acquire concentrated energetic structure, they generate localized substrate excitation energy (\(\Delta E_{\mathrm{sub}} = \frac{1}{2}\langle\delta\Psi, \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sub}}\delta\Psi\rangle\)). This energy redistribution gives rise to thermodynamic parameters like entropy (\(S\)) and temperature (\(T\)), defining the Transformation Domain (heat, energy exchange, and state changes).

Transformation to Continuum Transition (Agni Domain → Jala Domain):

When these energetic excitations become massive and densely distributed, individual tracking becomes impractical. Through mathematical coarse-graining over an intermediate scale (\(\ell \ll L \ll L_{\mathrm{macro}}\)), they form a collective density field \(\rho(x,\lambda)\) governed by the standard continuity equation (\(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\lambda} + \nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{v}) = 0\)). This establishes the Continuum Domain (fluid-like collective flow and conservation fields).

Continuum to Stable Material Organization Transition (Jala Domain → Pṛthvī Domain):

Finally, the continuum flow achieves permanent structure when it satisfies strict equilibrium (\(\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\Psi}\big\vert_{\Psi_0} = 0\)) and positive stability (\(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sub}} > 0\)). This locks the substrate configuration into persistent localization, yielding rest-mass energy (\(E_0 = Mc^2\)) and the Stable Material Organization Domain (solid matter and atomic stability).
