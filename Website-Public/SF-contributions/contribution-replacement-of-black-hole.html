Replacement of Black Hole with Finite Synthesis Anchor

The Synthesis Framework establishes that gravitational collapse does not terminate in a physical black-hole singularity. The gravitational-collapse process is retained, but its terminal state is replaced by a finite Synthesis Anchor. The Synthesis Anchor is a finite, dynamically determined state of the substrate in which recursive saturation arrests further gravitational compression. This establishes a finite endpoint of gravitational collapse and replaces the singular terminal ontology with a physically defined structure having a non-zero radius.

Gravitational Collapse and Its Terminal State

The Synthesis Framework does not replace gravitational collapse with a different physical process. Gravitational attraction, compression, and relativistic collapse remain the governing processes. The fundamental change occurs at the terminal stage of collapse: instead of continuing toward infinite density and divergent curvature, the collapsing system reaches a finite substrate equilibrium.

The structural sequence is

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Gravitational Collapse} &\rightarrow \text{Increasing Compression}\\ &\rightarrow \text{Recursive Substrate Response}\\ &\rightarrow \text{Saturation}\\ &\rightarrow \text{Collapse Arrest}\\ &\rightarrow \text{Finite Synthesis Anchor}. \end{aligned} \]

The Black-Hole Terminal State Replaced

In the conventional description, sufficiently strong gravitational collapse is associated with the formation of a black hole and ultimately with a singularity in the classical interior description. The Synthesis Framework establishes a different terminal structure. The collapsing system reaches a finite Synthesis Anchor before the substrate can be compressed to zero spatial scale.

Thus, the replacement is specifically a replacement of the terminal physical state, not a rejection of the gravitational-collapse process. The exterior gravitational field and the strong-field character of the collapsing system remain part of the gravitational description, while the interior terminal state is determined by substrate saturation.

The Finite Synthesis Anchor

The Synthesis Anchor is the finite terminal configuration produced when recursive substrate dynamics arrest gravitational collapse. It possesses a non-zero radius determined by the mass of the collapsing system and the vacuum moduli of the substrate.

The Anchor radius is

\[ r_A=\left(\frac{8\pi G^2M}{KP}\right)^{1/3}. \]

For finite mass \(M\), positive vacuum stiffness \(K\), and positive synthesis pressure \(P\), the Anchor radius is finite and non-zero. The collapse therefore terminates at a definite physical scale rather than at \(r=0\).

Role of Vacuum Stiffness and Synthesis Pressure

The finite endpoint is generated by the two fundamental vacuum moduli of the substrate. Vacuum stiffness \(K\) provides resistance to structural compression, while synthesis pressure \(P\) provides the restoring substrate response associated with the synthesis dynamics.

The existence condition is

\[ \begin{aligned} K&>0,\qquad P>0\\ &\Rightarrow \frac{P}{K}>0\\ &\Rightarrow \text{finite gravitational endpoint}. \end{aligned} \]

The Synthesis Anchor is therefore not an externally imposed cutoff. It is the finite terminal state generated by the intrinsic structural properties of the substrate.

Recursive Saturation

As gravitational compression increases, the substrate response becomes progressively stronger. At the terminal regime, recursive self-interaction reaches the saturation condition at which additional compression no longer produces unrestricted contraction. The gravitational collapse is consequently arrested by the substrate's own structural dynamics.

The mechanism can be expressed as

\[ \Sigma_{\mu\nu}=0 \rightarrow \text{strong-field response} \rightarrow \text{recursive saturation} \rightarrow \text{collapse arrest} \rightarrow \text{Synthesis Anchor}. \]

The finite Anchor is therefore the physical manifestation of the substrate reaching its terminal recursive equilibrium under gravitational compression.

Removal of the Singularity

The replacement of the black-hole terminal state with a finite Synthesis Anchor removes the singularity from the physical description. The collapse no longer requires a state of infinite density, infinite curvature, or zero radius. Instead, the gravitational system reaches a finite structural configuration determined by its mass and the substrate parameters.

The singularity is consequently replaced by a finite physical endpoint rather than treated as an ultimate constituent of reality.

Scale Independence

The Synthesis Anchor is not restricted to stellar collapse. The same substrate mechanism applies whenever a gravitational system reaches the saturation regime. The resulting Anchor radius scales with the mass of the system while the underlying resolution mechanism remains unchanged.

The Anchor hierarchy is therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Stellar Anchor} &\rightarrow \text{Galactic Anchor}\\ &\rightarrow \text{Universe-Scale Anchor}. \end{aligned} \]

This establishes a scale-independent terminal structure for gravitational collapse, governed by the same substrate dynamics at every scale.

Continuity with Gravitational Physics

The Synthesis Anchor does not require the abandonment of the established gravitational description in its appropriate regimes. Weak-field gravity, relativistic gravitational dynamics, and strong-field collapse remain continuous stages of one gravitational process. The new structure appears at the terminal stage where substrate saturation becomes dominant.

The complete sequence is therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Weak-Field Gravity} &\rightarrow \text{Relativistic Gravity}\\ &\rightarrow \text{Strong-Field Collapse}\\ &\rightarrow \text{Recursive Saturation}\\ &\rightarrow \text{Finite Synthesis Anchor}. \end{aligned} \]

Physical Implication

The replacement of the black-hole terminal state with a finite Synthesis Anchor changes the physical interpretation of gravitational collapse. A collapsed object is not required to terminate in an infinitely compressed state. Its terminal structure is a finite substrate configuration whose size, dynamics, and internal properties are determined by the Synthesis Framework.

The result provides a single physical endpoint for gravitational collapse while preserving the gravitational process that produces it.

Original Contribution

The replacement of the black-hole terminal state with a finite Synthesis Anchor is an original contribution of the Synthesis Framework. It establishes a finite endpoint of gravitational collapse directly from substrate dynamics, with the Anchor radius determined by

\[ r_A=\left(\frac{8\pi G^2M}{KP}\right)^{1/3}. \]

The result transforms gravitational collapse from a process terminating in a singularity into a finite, structurally determined process terminating in a Synthesis Anchor.

Structural Relation

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Gravitational Collapse} &\rightarrow \text{Black-Hole Regime}\\ &\rightarrow \text{Recursive Saturation}\\ &\rightarrow \text{Finite Synthesis Anchor}\\ &\rightarrow \text{No Physical Singularity}. \end{aligned} \]
