Prediction 1: Independent Derivation of the Acceleration Scale \(a_0\)

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Structural Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Vacuum Stiffness \(K=11.350\), normalized Synthesis Pressure \(P=0.6900\), physical Synthesis Pressure \(P_{\mathrm{phys}}\approx3.34\times10^{-36}\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}\), speed of light \(c\), and substrate stiffness length scale \(\ell_K=1/K\).
  • Core Mechanism: The characteristic acceleration scale is derived directly from the dimensional structure of the physical substrate pressure. Since \[ [P]=T^{-2}, \qquad [c]=LT^{-1}, \qquad [a_0]=LT^{-2}, \] the natural dimensional construction is \[ a_0=c\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{phys}}}. \] With \[ P_{\mathrm{phys}} \approx 3.34\times10^{-36}\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}, \] this gives \[ a_0 = (3.0\times10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}) (1.83\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}) \approx 5.5\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}. \] The independently derived vacuum stiffness \[ K\approx11.350 \] defines the corresponding substrate stiffness length scale \[ \ell_K=\frac{1}{K} \approx 1.45\times10^{25}\,\mathrm{m}. \] Thus \(P\) determines the characteristic acceleration magnitude, while \(K\) determines the substrate stiffness length scale.
  • Observable / Test: Empirically measured transition accelerations across diverse galactic dynamical systems, compared with the substrate-derived value \[ a_0\approx5.5\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}. \]
  • Localized Falsification: A systematic departure of the observed universal transition acceleration from the value determined by \[ a_0=c\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{phys}}}, \] when \(P_{\mathrm{phys}}\) is independently fixed by the substrate derivation, requiring an independent acceleration parameter, falsifies this specific acceleration-scale prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 2, 3, or 4.

Prediction 2: High-Redshift Kinematic Hardening (\(z>5\))

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative High-Redshift Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Present acceleration scale \(a_0\), redshift \(z\), background baryonic density \(\rho_b(z)\), metric-extension factor \(\chi(z)\), present metric factor \(\chi_0\), and the evolving substrate acceleration scale \(a_0(z)\).
  • Core Mechanism: The characteristic acceleration threshold evolves with the cosmological substrate background according to \[ a_0(z) = a_0(1+z)^3 \left( \frac{\chi_0}{\chi(z)} \right). \] The background density evolves as \[ \rho_b(z) = \rho_{b0}(1+z)^3. \] The substrate therefore predicts increasing restorative response during the high-redshift, strongly compressed phase of cosmic evolution. At \(z=5\), the density contribution alone gives \[ \frac{a_0(5)}{a_0} = (1+5)^3 = 216, \] before the metric-extension correction \(\chi_0/\chi(5)\). The resulting high-redshift structural hardening predicts enhanced internal kinematics in low-mass early galaxies, with the derived framework targeting a \(15\%\)--\(35\%\) enhancement in the velocity plateau: \[ \sigma_v(z>5) > \sigma_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}(z>5). \]
  • Observable / Test: High-redshift spectroscopic measurements of dwarf-galaxy internal kinematics and rotation profiles at \(z>5\), testing the predicted evolution of \(a_0(z)\), enhanced central velocity dispersions, and elevated or flatter rotational-velocity plateaus relative to equivalent zero-evolution baselines.
  • Localized Falsification: Observations systematically showing no substrate-induced elevation of the characteristic acceleration scale or no corresponding kinematic enhancement at the predicted redshifts falsify this specific high-redshift kinematic-hardening prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 1, 3, or 4.

Prediction 3: Cosmic Void Expansion Divergence and Anisotropic Shear Profiles

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Cosmological-Structure Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Hubble constant \(H_0\), matter density parameter \(\Omega_m\), radiation density parameter \(\Omega_r\), Synthesis Pressure \(P\), redshift \(z\), baryonic density \(\rho_b\), and the substrate pressure profile governing deep-void dynamics.
  • Core Mechanism: In deep cosmic voids, where \[ \rho_b\rightarrow0, \] the stabilizing matter-density constraint weakens and the substrate pressure balance relaxes. The resulting local void expansion is governed by \[ H_{\mathrm{void}}(z) = \sqrt{ H_0^2 \left[ \Omega_m(1+z)^3 + \Omega_r(1+z)^4 + (1-\Omega_m-\Omega_r)\tanh(Pz) \right] }. \] The same evolving substrate branch produces spatial gradients \[ \nabla x_{\gamma_S} \] at void--supercluster transition boundaries, generating characteristic anisotropic weak-lensing shear structures.
  • Observable / Test: Large-scale structure mapping, direct void-expansion measurements, and cosmic weak-lensing observations, testing both the predicted local expansion excess and the characteristic shear structure concentrated around void--supercluster boundaries. For the present deep-void limit, \[ H_{\mathrm{void}} \approx 1.042\,H_0, \] corresponding to a predicted \(4.2\%\) local expansion excess relative to the global background.
  • Localized Falsification: Deep voids systematically exhibiting no predicted local expansion divergence and no corresponding substrate-induced boundary shear falsify this specific void-expansion prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 1, 2, or 4.

Prediction 4: Symmetric Gravitational Lensing and Photon-Baryon Tracking

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Gravitational-Lensing Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Covariantly derived substrate stress-energy tensor \(\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{sub}}\), substrate-generated gravitational potential, dynamical potential \(\Phi_{\mathrm{dynamical}}\), lensing potential \(\Phi_{\mathrm{lensing}}\), and lensing slip parameter \(\eta\).
  • Core Mechanism: The covariantly derived substrate stress-energy tensor is \[ \mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{sub}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{total}}} {\delta g^{\mu\nu}}. \] Its gauge-closed metric coupling produces the same substrate geometric structure for massive dynamical trajectories and null-geodesic propagation. Consequently, lensing and dynamical potentials share the same spatial substrate gradient. Defining \[ \eta = \frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{lensing}}} {\Phi_{\mathrm{dynamical}}}, \] the covariant reduction gives \[ \eta\equiv1.0000. \] The framework therefore predicts covariant photon-baryon dynamical synchronization.
  • Observable / Test: High-resolution strong-lensing observations of massive galaxy clusters compared directly with corresponding dynamical mass and potential reconstructions. The predicted relation is \[ \frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{lensing}}(r)} {\Phi_{\mathrm{dynamical}}(r)} \equiv 1.0000, \] so Einstein rings, multiple-image separations, and giant-arc distortions reproduce the substrate-derived dynamical mass profile without an independent lensing-sector correction.
  • Localized Falsification: Systematic observations establishing a non-unit lensing-to-dynamical potential ratio incompatible with \[ \eta\equiv1 \] falsify this specific symmetric-lensing prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 1, 2, or 3.
