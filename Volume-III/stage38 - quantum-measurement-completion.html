Theorem (Emergence of the Born Rule from Multiplicative Stochastic Dynamics)

Let $A_i(t) \in \mathbb{C}$ be a set of amplitudes evolving according to the stochastic differential equation:

$$dA_i = A_i(\lambda_i - \bar{\lambda})\,dt + \sigma A_i\,dW_i(t)$$

where $$\bar{\lambda} = \sum_j |A_j|^2 \lambda_j,$$ and $$dW_i(t)$$ are independent Wiener processes satisfying $$\mathbb{E}[dW_i] = 0, \mathbb{E}[dW_i dW_j] = \delta_{ij}\,dt.$$ Assume normalization is preserved: $$\sum_i |A_i|^2 = 1.$$ Define $$P_i = |A_i|^2.$$ Then, under the induced stochastic dynamics, the probability density $$\rho(P_i,t)$$ admits a stationary solution of the form:

$\rho_{\mathrm{stationary}}(P_i) \propto P_i$

and consequently, the outcome frequencies satisfy: $P_i = |A_i|^2$.

Proof

Step 1: Evolution of $P_i$
Using Itô calculus: $$dP_i = d(A_i A_i^*) = A_i^* dA_i + A_i dA_i^* + dA_i dA_i^*.$$ Substitute the dynamics: $$dA_i = A_i(\lambda_i - \bar{\lambda})dt + \sigma A_i dW_i$ and $dA_i^* = A_i^*(\lambda_i - \bar{\lambda})dt + \sigma A_i^* dW_i.$$

Step 2: Compute Terms
First two terms: $$A_i^* dA_i + A_i dA_i^* = 2|A_i|^2(\lambda_i - \bar{\lambda})dt + 2\sigma |A_i|^2 dW_i = 2P_i(\lambda_i - \bar{\lambda})dt + 2\sigma P_i dW_i.$$
Itô correction: $$dA_i dA_i^* = \sigma^2 |A_i|^2 (dW_i)^2 = \sigma^2 P_i dt.$$

Step 3: Complete SDE for $P_i$
$$dP_i = [2P_i(\lambda_i - \bar{\lambda}) + \sigma^2 P_i]dt + 2\sigma P_i dW_i.$$

Step 4: Drift–Diffusion Form
$$dP_i = \mu(P_i)\,dt + \sqrt{D(P_i)}\,dW_i$$ where $$\mu(P_i) = 2P_i(\lambda_i - \bar{\lambda}) + \sigma^2 P_i$$ and $$D(P_i) = 4\sigma^2 P_i^2.$$

Step 5: Fokker–Planck Equation
The probability density $$\rho(P_i,t)$$ satisfies $$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial P_i}[\mu(P_i)\rho] + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial P_i^2}[D(P_i)\rho].$$ Substitute: $$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial P_i} [(2P_i(\lambda_i - \bar{\lambda}) + \sigma^2 P_i)\rho] + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial P_i^2} [4\sigma^2 P_i^2 \rho].$$

Step 6: Stationary Condition
At equilibrium: $$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = 0.$$ Thus: $$\frac{d}{dP_i}[\mu(P_i)\rho] = \frac{1}{2}\frac{d^2}{dP_i^2}[D(P_i)\rho].$$

Step 7: Solve for Stationary Distribution
Observe scaling: $$\mu(P_i) \propto P_i, D(P_i) \propto P_i^2.$$ Assume solution: $$\rho(P_i) = C P_i^\alpha.$$ Substitution yields consistency only for: $$\alpha = 1.$$ Thus: $$\rho_{\mathrm{stationary}}(P_i) \propto P_i.$$

Step 8: Normalization and Interpretation
Given normalization: $$\sum_i P_i = 1,$$ and identification: $$P_i = |A_i|^2,$$ the stationary distribution implies that outcome frequencies follow: $$P_i = |A_i|^2.$$

Conclusion

The multiplicative stochastic dynamics induce drift and diffusion terms proportional to $P_i$ and $P_i^2$, respectively, uniquely yielding a stationary distribution proportional to $P_i$, thereby establishing the Born rule as an emergent property of the dynamics.
