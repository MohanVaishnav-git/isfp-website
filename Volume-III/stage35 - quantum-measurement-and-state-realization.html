Summary — Mathematical Structure of Stage 35

This stage establishes the mathematical description of probability, uncertainty, confinement, dispersion, and the minimum quantum action bound. The equations collectively describe how oscillatory excitations in the vacuum substrate produce the statistical structure observed in quantum mechanics.

1. Probability Density (Born Relation)

The probability density associated with a quantum excitation is defined by the squared amplitude of the wavefield:

$$P(x)=|\psi(x)|^2$$

where $\psi(x)$ is the substrate oscillation amplitude and $P(x)$ is the probability density of excitation. According to this framework, the probability distribution represents the spatial density of oscillatory energy within the vacuum substrate.

2. Gaussian Localization of Wave Packets

Localized excitations form Gaussian probability envelopes:

$$\psi(x)=A e^{-x^2/(2\sigma^2)}$$

which gives:

$$P(x)=A^2 e^{-x^2/\sigma^2}$$

where $\sigma$ is the localization scale. Vacuum stiffness determines the confinement scale $\sigma \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}$. Thus stronger stiffness produces sharper localization of probability peaks.

3. Uncertainty Relation

The fundamental uncertainty bound between position and momentum is:

$$\Delta x \Delta p \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}$$

This inequality defines the minimum phase-space area occupied by a quantum state. The boundary curve of allowed states is $\Delta p = \frac{\hbar}{2\Delta x}$, corresponding to the hyperbolic limit shown in the visualization.

4. Wavepacket Dispersion

The spatial width of a Gaussian wave packet evolves with time according to:

$$\sigma(t)=\sigma_0\sqrt{1+t^2}$$

or more generally:

$$\sigma(t)=\sqrt{\sigma_0^2+\left(\frac{\hbar t}{2m\sigma_0}\right)^2}$$

where $\sigma_0$ is the initial packet width. According to this framework the spreading arises from phase divergence between oscillatory substrate modes.

5. Minimum Action Constraint

The uncertainty relation implies a minimum quantum of action:

$$S \ge \hbar$$

where $S \sim \Delta x \Delta p$. This establishes Planck’s constant as the smallest physically meaningful action scale.

6. Structural Origin of Quantum Limits

According to this framework the structural parameters of the vacuum substrate determine the quantum scales. Vacuum stiffness determines spatial confinement $\sigma \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}$. Synthesis pressure introduces stochastic fluctuations $\Delta p \sim \sqrt{P}$. Combining the two gives an action scale:

$$S \sim \sigma \Delta p$$

which approaches the universal constant $S \rightarrow \hbar$ at structural equilibrium.

7. Phase-Space Interpretation

The minimum cell of phase space is $A_{cell}=2\pi\hbar$. No physical state can occupy a phase-space region smaller than this quantum cell. Thus the structure of phase space becomes discretized at the quantum scale.

Conclusion

Stage 35 establishes that probability, uncertainty, dispersion, and quantum limits arise from the mathematical structure of oscillatory excitations in the vacuum substrate. The statistical character of quantum mechanics emerges from the interaction between vacuum stiffness $K$ and synthesis pressure $P$, which together determine the localization scale, fluctuation strength, and minimum action governing quantum systems.
