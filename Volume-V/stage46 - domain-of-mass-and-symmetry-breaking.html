STAGE 46 — MATHEMATICAL SUMMARY

Mass, Symmetry Breaking, and Differentiation

1. Symmetric Starting Point

Begin with a symmetry-invariant Lagrangian:

$\mathcal{L} = |D_\mu \phi|^2 - V(\phi) - \frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$

Potential: $V(\phi) = \mu^2 |\phi|^2 + \lambda |\phi|^4, \quad \lambda > 0$

Symmetry: $\phi \rightarrow e^{i\alpha(x)} \phi$

2. Degenerate Vacuum Structure

For $\mu^2 < 0$, minimization gives:

$|\phi| = v = \sqrt{-\frac{\mu^2}{2\lambda}}$

Vacuum manifold: $\phi = v e^{i\theta}, \quad \theta \in [0,2\pi)$

Degenerate vacua: continuous symmetry preserved in potential

3. Instability of Symmetric State

At $\phi = 0$:

$\frac{d^2 V}{d\phi^2} = \mu^2 < 0$
Symmetric state is unstable (false vacuum)

4. Vacuum Selection (Spontaneous Breaking)

System settles at $|\phi| = v \neq 0$, resulting in $G \rightarrow H$.

Symmetry is preserved in laws but broken in the vacuum state.

5. Field Decomposition

$\phi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v + h(x)) e^{i\theta(x)/v}$

$h(x) \rightarrow$ radial mode (massive); $\theta(x) \rightarrow$ Goldstone mode (massless).

6. Gauge Interaction and Mass Generation

With $D_\mu = \partial_\mu - i g A_\mu$, the kinetic term yields:

$|D_\mu \phi|^2 \supset \frac{1}{2} g^2 v^2 A_\mu A^\mu \implies \boxed{ m_A = g v }$

7. Scalar Mass

$m_h^2 = 2\lambda v^2$

8. Sector Differentiation

Different fields $A_\mu^i$ with couplings $g_i$ yield:

$m_i = g_i v$
Different couplings $\Rightarrow$ Different masses

9. Emergence of Physical Diversity

$G \rightarrow H \Rightarrow \{\text{distinct field sectors}\}$

Reality = $\sum_i \text{Field}_i(m_i, g_i)$

10. Energy–Momentum Realization

Each sector carries its own energy-momentum tensor:

$T_{\mu\nu}^{(i)} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial (\partial^\mu \phi_i)} \partial_\nu \phi_i - g_{\mu\nu} \mathcal{L}_i$

🔷 COMPLETION THEOREM — STAGE 46

$\text{Given a symmetric field theory } \mathcal{L}[\phi] \text{ with } \mu^2 < 0,$
$\text{the vacuum becomes degenerate and the symmetric state unstable.}$
$\text{The system necessarily selects a vacuum } |\phi| = v \neq 0,$
$\text{thereby breaking } G \rightarrow H.$

$\text{Coupling of fields to the vacuum generates masses: } m_i = g_i v.$
$\text{Different couplings produce differentiated field sectors.}$

$\therefore$

Mass and physical diversity arise as necessary consequences of
spontaneous symmetry breaking and field–vacuum interaction.
