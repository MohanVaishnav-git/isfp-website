Quantum mechanics and general relativity are therefore complementary manifestations of one underlying substrate, emerging through distinct dynamical regimes and unified by a common first-principles formulation.

The Emergence of Quantum Dynamics ▾
Question ▸

How does the amplitude–phase decomposition of the substrate field lead to quantum dynamics, including the Schrödinger equation, the substrate quantum potential, and the effective Hamiltonian?

Answer ▸

The amplitude–phase decomposition expresses the substrate field in polar form, \(\Psi(x,t)=A(x,t)e^{i\theta(x,t)}\), with density \(\rho=A^2\). Substituting this decomposition into the Master Field Equation separates the dynamics into real and imaginary components, yielding a substrate Hamilton–Jacobi structure and a continuity relation governing coherent field evolution.

The real component gives the substrate Hamilton–Jacobi equation,

\[ (\nabla\theta)^2+\frac{P}{K}\rho-\frac{\nabla^2A}{A}=\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}. \]

The amplitude-gradient term defines the substrate quantum potential, \(Q_{\mathrm{sub}}=-\nabla^2A/A\), which captures the spatial variation of the field amplitude. The corresponding effective substrate Hamiltonian is structured as

\[ \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sub}}=-\frac{K}{\rho_b}\nabla^2+\frac{P}{K}\rho-\mathcal{N}_{\sigma}. \]

With the coefficient identification \(\frac{K}{\rho_b}=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\) and the phase-evolution relation \(\partial_t\theta=-H/\hbar\), the framework recovers Schrödinger dynamics,

\[ i\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t} =\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2+V_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)\right]\Psi. \]

The effective Hamiltonian incorporates substrate propagation, nonlinear density coupling, and fluctuation contributions.

Quantum dynamics thus emerges from the coherent phase evolution and spatial amplitude gradients of the underlying substrate field, rather than being introduced as an independent foundation.

The Quantum–Geometric Crossover ▾
Question ▸

What determines the transition between fluctuation-dominated quantum behaviour and equilibrium-dominated geometric behaviour, and how do the fluctuation-to-stiffness parameter and coherence length establish the relevant scale separation?

Answer ▸

The quantum–geometric crossover is governed by scale separation determined by substrate fluctuations, Vacuum Stiffness, and coarse-graining. The dimensionless fluctuation-to-stiffness parameter is defined as

\[ \epsilon=\frac{\sigma}{K}, \]

where \(\sigma=\sqrt{\langle(\delta\rho)^2\rangle}\) is the root-mean-square density-fluctuation amplitude and \(K\) is Vacuum Stiffness. It distinguishes three dynamical regimes: \(\epsilon\gg1\) corresponds to fluctuation-dominated quantum behaviour, \(\epsilon\sim1\) to an intermediate regime, and \(\epsilon\ll1\) to equilibrium-dominated geometric behaviour.

The intrinsic stiffness length is \(\ell_K=c_S/\sqrt{K}\), where \(c_S\) is the characteristic substrate propagation speed. Combining this scale with \(\epsilon\) gives the coherence length:

\[ L_c=C_c\frac{\ell_K}{\epsilon} =C_c\frac{c_S\sqrt{K}}{\sigma}, \]

where \(C_c\) is a dimensionless structural coefficient. This length establishes the characteristic spatial scale of the quantum–geometric crossover.

At macroscopic scales \(\ell\gg L_c\), spatial coarse-graining with a normalized averaging kernel \(W_\ell(x-x')\) suppresses microscopic fluctuations, and the coarse-grained density approaches the smooth equilibrium configuration:

\[ \rho_\ell(x,t)\longrightarrow\bar{\rho}(x). \]

This equilibrium density generates the emergent metric and spacetime curvature. At scales comparable to or below \(L_c\), substrate fluctuations remain dynamically significant and quantum behaviour dominates.

The fluctuation-to-stiffness parameter and coherence length thus govern the transition from microscopic quantum dynamics to macroscopic spacetime geometry within one unified substrate system.

The Operator-Valued Substrate and Quantum State Space ▾

\[ \hat{\Psi}(x,t)=\sum_k\hat{a}_k\phi_k(x,t),\qquad \hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(x,t)=\sum_k\hat{a}_k^{\dagger}\phi_k^*(x,t), \]

The operator-valued substrate thus incorporates quantum state space, observables, measurement, and canonical commutation relations into the same underlying physical description. The Quantum–Geometric Feedback Loop ▾

    \[ \rho(x,t)=\langle\psi|\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(x,t)\hat{\Psi}(x,t)|\psi\rangle. \]

The Three Quantitative Predictions ▾

This relation predicts a finite, substrate-determined coherence scale. The corresponding crossover separation is (\(\Delta x_c=L_c\)), providing a quantitative basis for testing the transition from coherent quantum behaviour to equilibrium-dominated geometry.

The third prediction establishes a closed feedback loop in which the operator-valued substrate field generates the density, the density determines the emergent metric, and that metric governs the subsequent evolution of the same field. Together, these experiments test three distinct consequences of the unified substrate: the parameter-determined coherence scale, the quantitative transfer of density fluctuations into spacetime geometry, and the feedback of emergent geometry on quantum evolution. The Significance of the Unification ▾

The fundamental conceptual change is that quantum dynamics and spacetime geometry are no longer treated as independent foundations of physical reality. Rather than beginning with quantum mechanics and general relativity as independently established frameworks and attempting to reconcile them, the unified substrate formulation derives both from a common physical foundation. \[ g_{\mu\nu} =\eta_{\mu\nu}\left(1+\frac{P}{K}\bar{\rho}\right). \]
