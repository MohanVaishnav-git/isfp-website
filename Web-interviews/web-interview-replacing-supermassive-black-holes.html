Beyond the Singularity — Replacing Supermassive Black Holes with Energetic Galactic Cores

24 October 2026

Introduction ▸

Interviewer: Editorial Desk, Institute of Structural Foundations of Physics (ISFP)

Interviewee: Mohan Vaishnav, Author and Researcher, ISFP, Ahmedabad, India

For decades, conventional astrophysics has interpreted the centres of major galaxies as supermassive black holes—extremely compact objects associated with event horizons and, in classical General Relativity, singularities. The Synthesis Framework advances a fundamental alternative: the Galactic Energetic Core, an active, nonlinear concentration of the vacuum substrate that produces extreme gravitational intensity, energetic plasma activity, and relativistic outflows without requiring a singularity or event horizon.

In this interview, Mohan Vaishnav, author and researcher at the Institute of Structural Foundations of Physics (ISFP), explains the physical principles behind this replacement, showing how substrate dynamics, mass-energy synthesis, and the hierarchical organization of critical substrate states provide a unified interpretation of galactic cores and their role in cosmic evolution.

The Black-Hole Interpretation and the Energetic Galactic Core ▾
Question ▸

Modern astrophysics treats the centres of major galaxies as supermassive black holes bounded by event horizons. Why does Synthesis Framework challenge this interpretation?

Answer ▸

The conventional black-hole interpretation describes the galactic centre as a collapsed mass whose gravitational field produces an event horizon and, in classical General Relativity, a singularity. The Synthesis Framework replaces this interpretation with an active, nonlinear Energetic Galactic Core formed through the dynamics of the vacuum substrate. The extreme gravity, intense radiation, and relativistic outflows observed at galactic centres arise from this core’s energetic and structural organization. The central object is therefore not a terminal gravitational sink but an active physical system whose substrate dynamics unify extreme gravity with continuous energetic activity.

How does the Synthesis Framework explain the internal structure and dynamics of an Energetic Galactic Core without invoking a gravitational singularity?

Rather than representing the terminal collapse of matter into a singularity, the Galactic Core expresses an active substrate state in which energetic concentration and extreme gravity arise from the same underlying field dynamics.

Early Cosmic Evolution and the Emergence of Massive Galaxies ▾
Question ▸

How does the Energetic Galactic Core explain the appearance of massive black-hole candidates and highly developed galaxies at very early cosmic epochs?

The conventional growth picture relies heavily on the accumulation of matter through accretion and hierarchical assembly. The Synthesis Framework introduces a deeper structural principle: energetic cores arise from the dynamics of the vacuum substrate and are not dependent solely on the gradual accumulation of pre-existing matter. Early massive galactic systems are therefore understood through the combined action of substrate synthesis and hierarchical cosmic organization, rather than through accretion alone.

Jets and High-Energy Emissions of Active Galactic Nuclei ▾
Question ▸

How does Synthesis Framework model explain the powerful jets and high-energy emissions of active galactic nuclei without relying on conventional black-hole and accretion-disk mechanisms?

Answer ▸

In conventional models, relativistic jets are commonly explained through magnetic processes powered by accretion and, in some models, the rotational energy of a black hole. According to the Synthesis Framework, jets and polar outflows arise from the active energetic dynamics of the Galactic Core. At the critical Electroweak state, the nonlinear substrate concentration generates extreme energetic conditions, while axial pressure gradients and electromagnetic organization channel energy and momentum into collimated relativistic outflows. Jets are therefore manifestations of the core’s active substrate dynamics, connecting its energetic concentration with plasma acceleration and the transfer of energy into the surrounding galaxy.

Galaxy Evolution and the M–σ Relation ▾
Question ▸

What does replacing supermassive black holes with Energetic Galactic Cores imply for galaxy evolution and the observed \(M\!-\!\sigma\) relation?

Answer ▸

The observed \(M\!-\!\sigma\) relation connects the central mass of a galaxy with the velocity dispersion of its stellar bulge, indicating a close relationship between the central object and the host galaxy’s evolution. According to the Synthesis Framework, this relationship reflects the deeper structural organization of the Galactic Core and its periphery. The core’s energetic state and substrate field generate the gravitational organization of the surrounding stellar system, while energy and momentum transfer regulate the surrounding plasma and galactic dynamics. The Core-to-Periphery Principle therefore interprets the central object and the host galaxy as dynamically connected expressions of a common substrate structure, rather than as independently evolving components linked only through accretion and feedback.

Event Horizon Telescope Observations and the Energetic Galactic Core ▾

The framework replaces the black-hole interpretation with a singularity-free energetic core, unifying the observed optical morphology, extreme gravity, and relativistic outflows through the dynamics of the vacuum substrate.
