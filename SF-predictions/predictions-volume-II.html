Status: Quantum Substrate — Three Predictions

Prediction 1: Finite High-Energy Excitation Boundary

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Structural Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Vacuum Stiffness \(K\), Synthesis Pressure \(P\), minimum substrate wavelength \(\lambda_{\min}(K,P)\), maximum wave number \(k_{\max}(K,P)\), maximum excitation frequency \(\omega_{\max}(K,P)\), and maximum excitation energy \(E_{\max}(K,P)\).
  • Core Mechanism: The physical structure of the substrate imposes a finite structural resolution. The minimum wavelength therefore satisfies \[ \lambda\geq\lambda_{\min}(K,P). \] Consequently, \[ k\leq k_{\max}(K,P), \] and the excitation frequency and energy possess finite upper bounds, \[ \omega\leq\omega_{\max}(K,P), \qquad E\leq E_{\max}(K,P). \] The substrate therefore does not permit an unlimited continuation of the excitation spectrum toward arbitrarily large wave number. The ultraviolet excitation domain terminates at a physically determined boundary: \[ k_{\max}=k_{\max}(K,P), \] \[ E_{\max}=E_{\max}(K,P). \] The complete boundary relation is \[ \lambda_{\min}(K,P) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; k_{\max}(K,P) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; E_{\max}(K,P). \]
  • Observable / Test: Precision searches for physical propagation or excitation approaching the derived substrate boundary, testing whether excitations continue to obey the unrestricted low-energy dispersion structure as their wavelength approaches \(\lambda_{\min}(K,P)\), and whether the predicted termination or modification occurs at \(k_{\max}(K,P)\) and \(E_{\max}(K,P)\).
  • Localized Falsification: Observation of physically propagating modes beyond the derived maximum wave number and energy while retaining the unrestricted continuation of the low-energy dispersion relation falsifies this specific finite high-energy excitation boundary prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 2 or 3.

Prediction 2: Discrete Resonance Spectrum and Forbidden Resonance Intervals

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Structural Prediction / Quantum-State Selection
  • Underlying Parameters: Vacuum Stiffness \(K\), Synthesis Pressure \(P\), effective organizational scale \(L\), allowed resonance wave numbers \(k_n\), substrate dispersion relation \(\omega(k;K,P)\), and Planck's constant \(\hbar\).
  • Core Mechanism: Stable particle states arise only from self-consistent standing resonances. For a resonance confined to an effective organizational scale \(L\), the allowed wave numbers satisfy \[ k_n=\frac{n\pi}{L}, \qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots \] and the corresponding frequencies are \[ \omega_n=\omega(k_n;K,P). \] The resulting energy spectrum is \[ E_n=\hbar\omega(k_n;K,P), \] or explicitly, \[ \{E_n\} = \left\{ \hbar\omega\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{L};K,P\right) \right\}. \] Mass follows from the persistent resonant energy: \[ M_n=\frac{E_n}{c^2}, \] giving the resonance mass spectrum \[ \{M_n\} = \left\{ \frac{\hbar}{c^2} \omega\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{L};K,P\right) \right\}. \] Energies lying between the allowed stable resonance branches do not correspond to persistent particle states. The substrate therefore imposes forbidden intervals in which no stable resonant organization exists.
  • Observable / Test: Precision searches for new stable or long-lived particle states and resonances, testing whether persistent states occupy the derived allowed resonance branches and whether stable fundamental states are absent from the corresponding forbidden intervals.
  • Localized Falsification: Observation of a stable or long-lived fundamental state at an energy belonging to a mathematically derived forbidden resonance interval falsifies this specific discrete resonance-spectrum prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 1 or 3.

Prediction 3: Quadratic Vacuum-Induced Metric Spectrum

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Structural Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Vacuum Stiffness \(K\), Synthesis Pressure \(P\), substrate fluctuation \(\delta\phi\), quadratic substrate-to-geometry response coefficient \(C(K,P)\), substrate fluctuation spectrum \(S_\phi(\omega)\), and derived geometric fluctuation spectrum \(S_g(\omega)\).
  • Core Mechanism: Microscopic substrate fluctuations generate a quadratic geometric response: \[ \delta g_{\mu\nu} = C(K,P)(\delta\phi)^2. \] After removal of the constant mean contribution, the metric fluctuation correlation function is \[ C_g^{\mathrm{fl}}(\tau) = 2C^2(K,P)C_\phi^2(\tau), \] where \[ C_\phi(\tau) = \left\langle \delta\phi(t)\delta\phi(t+\tau) \right\rangle. \] Fourier transformation gives \[ S_g(\omega) = \frac{2C^2(K,P)}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} S_\phi(\omega') S_\phi(\omega-\omega') \,d\omega'. \] Thus the geometric fluctuation spectrum is the self-convolution of the underlying substrate fluctuation spectrum: \[ S_g(\omega) \propto S_\phi*S_\phi. \] The finite excitation boundary propagates into the geometric spectrum. If \[ S_\phi(\omega)=0 \qquad \text{for} \qquad |\omega|>\omega_{\max}(K,P), \] then \[ S_g(\omega)=0 \qquad \text{for} \qquad |\omega|>2\omega_{\max}(K,P). \] Therefore the geometric spectral boundary is \[ \omega_{\max}^{(g)} = 2\omega_{\max}(K,P). \] The framework consequently predicts both a distinctive quadratic spectral construction and a finite upper spectral boundary for vacuum-induced geometric fluctuations.
  • Observable / Test: Precision measurements of stochastic metric or gravitational-wave backgrounds over sufficiently broad frequency ranges, testing whether the measured geometric fluctuation spectrum follows the derived quadratic convolution \[ S_g(\omega)\propto S_\phi*S_\phi \] and terminates at the predicted geometric spectral boundary \[ \omega_{\max}^{(g)} = 2\omega_{\max}(K,P). \]
  • Localized Falsification: A measured stochastic geometric spectrum that cannot be represented by the derived quadratic convolution of the substrate spectrum, or a confirmed continuation of the geometric spectrum beyond the predicted boundary \(\omega_{\max}^{(g)}=2\omega_{\max}(K,P)\), falsifies this specific quadratic vacuum-induced metric-spectrum prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 1 or 2.
