The First-Principles Recovery of Quantum Measurement from the Substrate

Volume III : Quantum Measurement Domain

Status: Quantum Measurement — Three Predictions

Prediction 1: Fundamental Substrate-Induced Decoherence Floor

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Structural Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Vacuum Stiffness \(K\), Synthesis Pressure \(P\), system--substrate coupling \(g\), characteristic substrate frequency \(\Omega\), and substrate decoherence coefficient \(\lambda_{\min}\).
  • Core Mechanism: Decoherence possesses an intrinsic substrate contribution arising from recursive phase redistribution within the coupled substrate organization. The phase structure is \[ \Phi = \Phi_q + \Phi_d + \sum_i\Phi_i, \] where the detector and progressively larger substrate domains participate in the evolving recursive equilibrium. Because microscopic substrate fluctuations remain intrinsically active, external isolation cannot eliminate the complete decoherence contribution. The substrate therefore imposes a fundamental non-zero decoherence floor: \[ \lambda_{\min} \approx \left( \frac{P}{K} \right) \frac{g^2}{\Omega}. \] Consequently, \[ \lambda \geq \lambda_{\min} > 0. \] The lower bound is quantitatively determined by the fundamental substrate ratio \(P/K\), the system--substrate coupling \(g\), and the characteristic substrate frequency \(\Omega\).
  • Observable / Test: Residual decoherence rates or purity decay in ultra-high-\(Q\) macroscopic optomechanical resonators, levitated nanoparticles, mechanical quantum resonators, and macroscopic superposition experiments operated under progressively improved cryogenic, vacuum, and isolation conditions. The measurement must determine the residual decoherence after independently characterized environmental contributions have been reduced to their experimental minima and test its dependence on \(P/K\), \(g\), and \(\Omega\).
  • Localized Falsification: Progressive environmental isolation that causes the total residual decoherence rate to approach zero without any persistent substrate-correlated lower bound falsifies this specific fundamental substrate-induced decoherence-floor prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 2 or 3.

Prediction 2: Finite-Time Relaxation to the Born Equilibrium

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Dynamical Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Fokker--Planck relaxation operator \(\mathcal{L}\), relaxation eigenvalues \(\Gamma_n\), slowest non-zero relaxation rate \(\Gamma_{\min}\), relaxation time \(\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}\), ensemble probability density \(\rho(P,t)\), and Born equilibrium \(\rho_0(P)\propto P\).
  • Core Mechanism: The Fokker--Planck dynamics establishes a finite-time relaxation of a deliberately prepared non-equilibrium measurement ensemble toward the unique stationary Born equilibrium. Writing \[ \rho(P,t) = \rho_0(P) + \delta\rho(P,t), \] with \[ \rho_0(P) = \mathcal{N}P, \] the transient deviation satisfies \[ \frac{\partial\,\delta\rho}{\partial t} = -\mathcal{L}\,\delta\rho. \] Expansion in relaxation eigenmodes gives \[ \delta\rho(P,t) = \sum_n c_n f_n(P)e^{-\Gamma_n t}, \] where \[ \Gamma_n>0. \] The slowest non-zero mode defines the characteristic relaxation time: \[ \tau_{\mathrm{relax}} = \frac{1}{\Gamma_{\min}}. \] Consequently, sufficiently near equilibrium, \[ \rho(P,t) \simeq \rho_0(P) + c_1f_1(P)e^{-t/\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}}. \] The long-time limit is \[ \lim_{t\rightarrow\infty} \rho(P,t) = \rho_0(P) \propto P. \] The equilibrium probability-law exponent remains fixed at \[ \alpha_{\mathrm{Born}}=2, \] while \(\Gamma_{\min}\) determines the rate at which the initially non-equilibrium ensemble approaches that equilibrium. The prediction is therefore a finite substrate-determined dynamical relaxation toward the Born law rather than a modification of the Born exponent.
  • Observable / Test: Time-dependent probability distributions in ultra-fast quantum measurement cycles, repeated weak measurements, rapid sequential state tomography, and controlled measurement ensembles in superconducting qubits, trapped ions, photonic systems, and other high-coherence quantum platforms. Measurements should resolve time intervals comparable to or shorter than the predicted relaxation time \(\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}\).
  • Localized Falsification: Deliberately prepared non-equilibrium measurement ensembles that exhibit no finite-time relaxation toward the uniquely derived Born equilibrium, or whose relaxation occurs without the predicted finite substrate-determined characteristic timescale, falsify this specific finite-time relaxation-to-Born-equilibrium prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 1 or 3.

Prediction 3: Deterministic Substrate Zero-Point Noise Power Spectral Density

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Spectral Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Substrate field fluctuations \(\delta M\), \(\delta C\), and \(\delta N\), collective fluctuation field \(\eta(t)\), Vacuum Stiffness \(K\), Synthesis Pressure \(P\), oscillator characteristic frequency \(\omega_0\), and displacement Power Spectral Density \(S_{xx}(\omega)\).
  • Core Mechanism: Measurement fluctuations originate from collective microscopic interactions among the constituent substrate fields, \[ \delta M, \qquad \delta C, \qquad \delta N. \] Their collective contribution produces the effective fluctuation field \[ \eta(t) = \alpha\,\delta M + \beta\,\delta C + \gamma\,\delta N, \] whose equilibrium correlation function is \[ \left\langle \eta(t)\eta(t') \right\rangle = \frac{P}{K} \delta(t-t'). \] For an oscillator of characteristic frequency \(\omega_0\) coupled to this substrate fluctuation field, the predicted displacement Power Spectral Density is \[ S_{xx}(\omega) = \frac{2}{\pi} \left( \frac{P}{K} \right) \frac{1}{\omega^2+\omega_0^2}. \] The amplitude of this non-thermal spectral contribution is therefore fixed by the substrate fluctuation intensity \[ \sigma^2 = \frac{P}{K}. \] The prediction is consequently a definite frequency-dependent spectral form with an amplitude determined by the substrate ratio \(P/K\), rather than merely the existence of residual noise.
  • Observable / Test: Displacement-noise Power Spectral Density in advanced optomechanical force sensors, ground-state-cooled mechanical resonators, precision micro-cantilevers, and quantum-limited displacement detectors after thermal, seismic, electromagnetic, and other known environmental noise sources have been independently characterized and suppressed.
  • Localized Falsification: Precision measurements demonstrating that the residual displacement-noise spectrum is completely accounted for by conventional quantum and environmental noise sources, with no additional spectral component possessing the predicted \(P/K\) dependence and frequency form, falsify this specific deterministic substrate zero-point noise prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 1 or 2.
