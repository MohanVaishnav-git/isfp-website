Prediction 1: Quadratic Bound on Local Coupling-Constant Variations

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Structural Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Vacuum Stiffness \(K=11.350\), Synthesis Pressure \(P=0.6900\), local phase deviation \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{local}}\), phase-alignment condition \(\theta=\pi\), and structural coefficient \(\xi=P/(2K)\).
  • Core Mechanism: At the phase-alignment condition \[ \theta=\pi, \] the first-order phase variation of a physical coupling constant vanishes. For a small local deviation \[ \theta=\pi+\epsilon, \] the leading variation is therefore quadratic: \[ \frac{\Delta g}{g} = \xi\epsilon^2 + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^4), \] where \[ \xi = \frac{P}{2K}. \] Using \[ K=11.350, \qquad P=0.6900, \] gives \[ \xi = \frac{0.6900}{2(11.350)} = 0.030396 \approx 0.0304. \] The resulting quantitative bound is \[ \left| \frac{\Delta g}{g} \right| \leq 0.0304\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{local}}^2. \]
  • Observable / Test: Precision measurements of dimensionless coupling constants, including the fine-structure constant \(\alpha\) and the proton-to-electron mass ratio \(\mu\), together with high-precision atomic-clock comparisons, testing whether localized variations obey the predicted quadratic dependence on the relevant substrate perturbation.
  • Localized Falsification: Reproducible localized measurements establishing \[ \frac{\Delta g}{g} \propto \epsilon_{\mathrm{local}} \] rather than the predicted quadratic dependence, or measurements satisfying \[ \left| \frac{\Delta g}{g} \right| > 0.0304\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{local}}^2 \] under the corresponding substrate perturbation, falsify this specific quadratic coupling-variation prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 2 or 3.

Prediction 2: Structural Inflection in Galactic Rotation Curves

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Astrophysical Prediction
  • Underlying Parameters: Vacuum stiffness \(K(r)\), restorative gravitational acceleration \(a(r)\), characteristic acceleration threshold \(a_0\approx1.2\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), and the stiffness curvature \(d^2K/dr^2\).
  • Core Mechanism: The gravitational response attributed phenomenologically to dark matter arises from the restorative response of the substrate governed by vacuum stiffness: \[ a(r) = -\frac{dK}{dr}. \] The substrate therefore predicts a structural transition as the local acceleration approaches \[ a_0 \approx 1.2\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}. \] At this threshold, the stiffness profile possesses a structural inflection: \[ \frac{d^2K}{dr^2}=0. \] The corresponding observable signature is a localized structural feature in the rotation-curve transition region. In sufficiently resolved isolated disk galaxies and low-surface-brightness systems, the transition from the Newtonian regime toward the asymptotically flat rotation regime is predicted to exhibit a localized inflection or oscillatory plateau in the circular-velocity profile near \[ a(r)\approx a_0. \]
  • Observable / Test: High-resolution spatially resolved kinematic observations of individual isolated disk galaxies and low-surface-brightness galaxies, with particular examination of the acceleration interval surrounding \[ a_0 \approx 1.2\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}, \] testing for the predicted localized inflection, oscillatory plateau, or corresponding dispersion signature in the circular-velocity structure.
  • Localized Falsification: High-precision observations of a sufficiently large and diverse sample of isolated galaxies establishing that the transition through \[ a(r)\approx a_0 \] is systematically smooth and featureless, with no corresponding structural inflection or dispersion signature, falsify this specific substrate restorative-gravity prediction only. It does not, by itself, falsify Predictions 1 or 3.

Prediction 3: Redshift-Dependent Cosmic Expansion from Synthesis-Pressure Saturation

  • Classification: Genuinely Novel Quantitative Cosmological Prediction

  • Core Mechanism: The cosmic expansion rate is governed by the dynamical action of synthesis pressure: \[ H(z)^2 = H_0^2 \left[ \Omega_m(1+z)^3 + \Omega_r(1+z)^4 + \Phi(P,z) \right]. \] The first-order equilibrium closure gives the substrate saturation function \[ \Phi(P,z) = (1-\Omega_m-\Omega_r) \left( 1-e^{-Pz} \right). \] Since \[ P=0.6900, \] the predicted expansion contribution becomes \[ \Phi(z) = (1-\Omega_m-\Omega_r) \left( 1-e^{-0.6900z} \right). \] The complete expansion law is therefore \[ H(z)^2 = H_0^2 \left[ \Omega_m(1+z)^3 + \Omega_r(1+z)^4 + (1-\Omega_m-\Omega_r) \left( 1-e^{-0.6900z} \right) \right]. \] The synthesis-pressure contribution has the limiting behaviours \[ 1-e^{-0.6900z} \sim 0.6900z, \qquad z\ll1, \] and \[ 1-e^{-0.6900z} \rightarrow 1, \qquad z\gg1. \] Thus the predicted contribution evolves at low redshift and approaches a finite asymptotic saturation value at high redshift.
